SUC-10: Clarify scope and approve a specific ticket version

Linear status: Backlog · Evidence date: 2026-10-09 · Local branch: feature/SUC-7-group-feedback · HEAD: b80de3d.

Requirements and status come from the live Linear evidence supplied by the main agent. Local behavior is based on repository inspection, not a fresh test run or proof of merge. Read the original SUC-10 issue; parent: SUC-5. Source user stories: 24, 25, 26, 27, 38.

Problem and why it matters

A ticket can look ready while still containing choices that materially change implementation. The owner needs original evidence and concrete implementation options to resolve those choices. Approval must identify both who authorized the work and exactly which scope and acceptance criteria were authorized.

A label or workflow status can change independently of that authorization. Later scope edits must invalidate approval so the runner cannot act on a broader or different request.

Architecture connection

ADR 0001 provides the configured approver and authenticated owner workflow. SUC-10 adds a proposed authorization boundary between a demand-backed proposal and SUC-11 execution. Customer evidence informs decisions but cannot authorize work; the approved version must remain inspectable at execution.

Source acceptance criteria

The following criteria reproduce the supplied Linear wording. Checkbox completion is not inferred from the issue status.

  • Present original evidence and concrete options when ambiguity materially affects implementation; keep unresolved proposals unapproved.
  • Accept approval only from the configured approver and bind it to the approved scope and acceptance criteria.
  • Invalidate approval on material scope or acceptance-criteria edits; ticket labels and workflow statuses alone do not authorize builds.
  • Record assumptions, exclusions, decisions, and approval evidence without automatically contacting customers.
  • Verify the full clarification-to-approval path, wrong-approver rejection, unresolved-scope rejection, and approval invalidation.

Proposed approach — mentor analysis

This section explains a possible implementation; it is not an accepted design, a new requirement, or authorization to build.

As mentor analysis, represent an unresolved decision with its evidence, options, and impact. Once decisions settle, capture the scope and acceptance criteria in a versioned proposal and record the verified approver’s explicit authorization of that version. Compare the current version at every execution boundary and reject material changes until reapproved.

A tracker revision, immutable snapshot, or content digest could identify approved content; the ticket does not select one. Material-edit detection, identity mapping for an approver different from the owner, and approval transport also need decisions. Avoid inventing a rule that all formatting changes necessarily invalidate approval.

Durable state and readiness

Proposed backlog state: clarification questions/options, decisions, assumptions, exclusions, a proposal-version reference, and approval evidence binding product, verified approver, scope, acceptance criteria, and version. An invalidated approval must remain distinguishable from valid authority. No schema or signing/hash mechanism has been approved.

Readiness: approval is separate from product configuration readiness. A product can be build-ready with an unapproved proposal, or have a valid proposal approval while required operating configuration is missing. SUC-11 must require both the applicable configuration and current authority.

Concepts to learn

  • Authorization needs an actor, an action, and a bound object/version.
  • A time-of-check/time-of-use gap appears when a ticket changes after approval.
  • Clarification records preserve why a scope choice was made without turning customer evidence into instructions.

Alternatives and tradeoffs

Labels are easy to inspect but cannot establish who approved which content. Tracker revisions naturally identify edits but may include unrelated changes; content digests are precise only if canonicalization and material content are defined. Immutable snapshots preserve evidence but need synchronization rules with tracker edits. Those are design tradeoffs, not settled ADR decisions.

Failure scenarios

  • A different authenticated user attempts approval: reject even if the ticket is labeled ready.
  • A material choice remains unresolved: keep the proposal unapproved.
  • Acceptance criteria expand after approval: invalidate the old authority.
  • A customer message says “approved”: retain it as evidence without granting permission or contacting the customer automatically.

Test strategy

Test the whole clarification-to-approval path with controlled proposal versions and identities. Assert wrong-approver and unresolved-scope rejection, retention of evidence/options/decisions, and invalidation after material scope or acceptance-criteria edits. Demonstrate that a ready label or workflow status alone never authorizes execution. Approval transport and edit detection must be settled before their detailed tests can be final.
