SUC-14: Report stopped work and resume only with fresh approval

Linear status: Backlog · Evidence date: 2026-10-09 · Local branch: feature/SUC-7-group-feedback · HEAD: b80de3d.

Requirements and status come from the live Linear evidence supplied by the main agent. Local behavior is based on repository inspection, not a fresh test run or proof of merge. Read the original SUC-14 issue; parent: SUC-5. Source user stories: 33, 34.

Problem and why it matters

A stopped attempt can contain useful changes while leaving failed checks, scope blockers, or exhausted limits. The owner needs an honest report and concrete decisions before more work begins. Resuming is another authorized attempt, not an automatic retry that quietly extends the old permission or budget.

Architecture connection

SUC-12 supplies the review evidence and unfinished-work report. SUC-10’s version-bound authority and SUC-11’s bounded execution remain relevant when a continuation starts. The persistent operating contract in ADR 0001 still governs repository access, restrictions, schedule, and limits.

Source acceptance criteria

The following criteria reproduce the supplied Linear wording. Checkbox completion is not inferred from the issue status.

  • Preserve draft work and report completed changes, failed checks, blockers, remaining work, and resource usage.
  • Stop on scope expansion or requirements that materially change implementation, with concrete decisions requested from the owner.
  • Require fresh approval before continuation; material scope changes require approval of the revised ticket version.
  • Assign a distinct continuation attempt, validate applicable execution limits, and prevent duplicate resumptions.
  • Verify stop-to-report-to-reapproval-to-resume behavior, including failed checks, exhausted limits, scope expansion, and attempted unapproved continuation.

Proposed approach — mentor analysis

This section explains a possible implementation; it is not an accepted design, a new requirement, or authorization to build.

As mentor analysis, preserve the original attempt outcome and draft, then present completed work, remaining work, failed checks, resource use, and specific scope decisions. Require a new explicit approval for continuation, including approval of a revised ticket version if scope materially changes. Link a distinct continuation attempt to the stopped attempt and route it through the same guarded runner.

The budget model for continuations is unresolved: per-attempt, per-weekend, cumulative, or another policy cannot be silently chosen. Fresh approval does not by itself define new limits or an exception to the scheduler’s at-most-one rule. That interaction belongs in the open questions.

Durable state and readiness

Proposed backlog state: an immutable or otherwise auditable stopped-attempt outcome, progress/draft reference, failed checks, blockers, usage, remaining work, continuation approval, and a distinct linked continuation identity. Exact schema, attempt lineage, and concurrency protocol are not approved.

Readiness: a stopped attempt does not make a profile ready or unready automatically. Continuation requires fresh authority and applicable configuration/limits at the time of execution. Material scope change invalidates the relevance of an old version’s approval; a retry request cannot stand in for reapproval.

Concepts to learn

  • A retry repeats an operation; continuation authorizes additional work after a meaningful stop.
  • Attempt lineage explains which approval and resource usage belong to each piece of work.
  • A scope blocker should become an explicit owner decision rather than an undocumented implementation assumption.

Alternatives and tradeoffs

Automatically restarting is convenient but violates fresh-approval requirements. Reusing the original attempt identity hides additional work and complicates audit. Distinct linked attempts preserve history but require clear budget and weekend policy. Copying the old cap into every attempt could unintentionally bypass an overall spending constraint; the policy is not settled.

Failure scenarios

  • A failed check prompts automatic continuation without approval: deny the new work.
  • A scope expansion is discovered: stop and request concrete choices before proceeding.
  • Two resume requests reference one continuation approval: prevent duplicate attempts.
  • A previous cap was exhausted: fresh approval alone must not be treated as proof of applicable remaining allowance.

Test strategy

Use controlled runner, usage, approval, and validation integrations to exercise stop → report → reapproval → resume. Cover failed checks, exhausted runtime/spend, material scope expansion, wrong/unapproved continuation, and duplicate resumes. Assert draft preservation, a distinct linked attempt, explicit owner decisions, and enforcement of the eventual continuation-limit policy. Use a controllable clock for window-sensitive cases.

Explain it back

  1. How does continuation differ from retrying a failed request?
  2. Why does a material scope change require a revised ticket-version approval?
  3. What must the stop report tell the owner before fresh approval is meaningful?
  4. Which unresolved budget and scheduling choices could a careless resume implementation bypass?
